## The blinds lose money, and that's normal

You pay the [[blinds|blinds]] every round, and you'll be [[out-of-position|out of position]] after the flop. Every player loses money from the blinds over time. The goal is to **lose as little as possible**.

## The big blind gets a discount

When someone opens to 2.5bb, the BB has already paid 1bb. To call, you put in 1.5bb more:

- Pot after your call: 2.5 (raiser) + 0.5 (SB) + 2.5 (you) = 5.5bb
- You need: 1.5 ÷ 5.5 ≈ **27% [[equity|equity]]**

That's cheap. Many hands have 27% or more against a wide range, so the BB [[defend|defends]] a lot, especially against late opens.

This calculation is called [[pot-odds|pot odds]]. You'll use it again later in the app.

## Defend more against later raisers

| Raiser | BB plays about |
|---|---|
| Early (UTG–MP) | 17% of hands |
| Middle (LJ–HJ) | 23% |
| Late (CO–BTN) | 47% |
| SB | 53% |

A button open is often a [[steal|steal]] with a weak hand. Fight back by calling with most suited hands and many offsuit ones.

## The small blind: 3-bet or fold

The SB has a smaller discount (it already paid only 0.5bb) and is out of position. Worse, the BB is still behind it and can [[squeeze|squeeze]].

So from the SB, mostly **[[3-bet|3-bet]] or fold**. Flat calling from the SB is a common, expensive mistake. In this app, SB 3-bets are 4× the open (10bb against 2.5bb), because you'll be out of position.

## Big blind vs small blind

When the SB opens, the BB has position for the whole hand. Defend very wide and 3-bet often.

## Key points

- Blinds lose money. Aim to lose less.
- BB: you need about 27% equity to call a 2.5bb open. Defend wide, widest vs late openers.
- SB: 3-bet or fold, rarely call.
