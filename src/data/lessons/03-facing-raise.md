## Your three options

Someone [[open|opened]] before you. You can:

- **Fold**: most of the time.
- **Call** (a [[flat|flat call]]): see a flop with a hand that plays well.
- **[[3-bet|3-bet]]**: re-raise. In this app that's 3× the open when you'll be [[in-position|in position]] (7.5bb against a 2.5bb open).

## Respect the raiser's seat

An open tells you something. A UTG open is a strong range of about 11% of hands. A button open could be almost half of all hands.

So the question isn't "is my hand good?" but **"is my hand good against their range?"** KQo is a fine hand, but against a UTG open it's often [[dominated|dominated]] by AK, AQ and KK. Fold it.

> You need a stronger hand to call a raise than to open yourself. The raiser has already shown strength.

## What to 3-bet

- **For [[value|value]]**: hands that are ahead of their range, like QQ+ and AK against an early open. Add JJ, AQ and more as the opener gets later.
- **As a [[bluff|bluff]]**: a few hands like A5s–A4s. The ace [[blocker|blocks]] their AA and AK, and the hand can still make a [[wheel|wheel]] or the nut flush if called.

## What to call

Hands that do well after the flop when you're in position:

- **Pairs**: they can flop a [[set|set]] and win a big pot.
- **Suited broadways and suited aces**: AJs, KQs, QJs.
- **[[suited-connector|Suited connectors]]**: mostly against later, wider opens.

Avoid offsuit hands that are often dominated, like KJo or QTo.

## Players behind you

If you call, the players behind you can still re-raise. That's called a [[squeeze|squeeze]], and it's why middle seats play tighter than the button in this spot.

## Key points

- Judge your hand against the raiser's range, not on its own.
- 3-bet your best hands plus a few suited-ace bluffs.
- Call with pairs and suited hands that play well. Fold dominated offsuit hands.
