## The situation

You [[open|opened]], and a player behind you [[3-bet|3-bet]]. Now you can fold, call, or [[4-bet|4-bet]] (about 2.2× their 3-bet).

## Don't marry your open

Your opening range has strong hands at the top and marginal hands at the bottom. Against a 3-bet, **fold the bottom part**. In this app you continue with roughly 40% of your UTG opens, about 60% of CO opens against a blind 3-bet, and only about 35% of button opens, because the button opens so many weak hands.

> Opening a hand is not a promise to play it to the end.

## Which hands continue

- **4-bet for [[value|value]]**: KK+ and AK almost always. QQ and AK more often as the 3-bettor's range gets wider.
- **4-bet bluffs**: a few hands like A5s–A4s. The ace [[blocker|blocks]] AA and AK.
- **Call**: pairs, strong suited aces, suited broadways. Hands that won't be [[dominated|dominated]] too often.
- **Fold**: weak offsuit hands like AJo vs an early 3-bet, small suited connectors, weak suited kings.

## Position changes everything

- **3-bet from the blinds**: you'll be [[in-position|in position]] after the flop. You can call wider.
- **3-bet from a player behind you who isn't a blind** (e.g. you open CO, BTN 3-bets): you'll be [[out-of-position|out of position]]. Call tighter.

## Who 3-bet matters too

A player who 3-bets your UTG open usually has a strong hand. A blind 3-betting a button open is often fighting back against a [[steal|steal]] with a wide range. Continue more against wide 3-bettors.

## Key points

- Fold the weakest part of your opening range.
- 4-bet KK+ and AK, plus a few suited-ace bluffs.
- Call wider in position, tighter out of position.

Your drill: **facing a 3-bet from every opening seat**. Score 16 of 20 to finish the preflop course.
