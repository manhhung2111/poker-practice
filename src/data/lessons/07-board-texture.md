## Why bet?

When it's checked to you, a bet needs a reason. There are only two:

- **[[value|Value]]**: worse hands will call. You're ahead and want more chips in the pot.
- **[[bluff|Bluff]]**: better hands will fold. You're behind and want them to give up.

A bet with neither reason just loses chips.

## Bet your strong hands

With about 60% [[equity|equity]] or more against their likely hands, bet. Checking gives free cards and misses value, especially on the river, your last chance to get paid.

## Check your medium hands

Hands like middle pair have [[showdown-value|showdown value]]: they win sometimes if nobody bets. If you bet, worse hands fold and better hands call, which is the opposite of what you want. Check, and get to showdown cheaply.

## Read the board: wet or dry

The [[wet-board|board texture]] changes everything.

| Board | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| K♠ 7♦ 2♣ | Dry | No flush draws, few straight draws |
| A♠ A♦ 6♣ | Dry | Paired: few strong hands |
| K♥ 9♥ 3♣ | Medium | One flush draw |
| J♥ T♥ 8♣ | Wet | Flush and straight draws everywhere |

- **Dry boards** favor the preflop raiser, who has more big pairs and strong aces. A small [[c-bet|c-bet]] (⅓ to ½ pot) works well, even without a hand.
- **Wet boards** hit the caller's suited and connected hands. C-bet less often, and when you bet for value, bet bigger (½ to ¾ pot) to charge the draws.

## Semi-bluffs

A [[semi-bluff|semi-bluff]] is a bet with a [[draw|draw]]. It wins two ways: they fold now, or you hit your draw later. Draws are the best hands to bluff with.

## Don't bluff a crowd

Against one opponent, a bluff only has to get one player to fold. Against three or more, someone usually has something. Without a strong hand or a draw, check into a crowd.

## Key points

- Bet for value with strong hands (about 60%+ equity).
- Check medium hands: they have showdown value.
- Dry board: small c-bets. Wet board: c-bet less, value bet bigger.
- Bluff with draws, heads-up. Don't bluff into many players.
