## Nine seats, one order

A 9-max table has nine seats. The [[button|dealer button]] moves one seat clockwise every hand, so every seat gets a name relative to it:

| Seat | Name | Group |
|---|---|---|
| UTG | [[utg|Under the Gun]] | Early |
| UTG+1 | Under the Gun +1 | Early |
| MP | Middle Position | Early |
| LJ | [[lojack|Lojack]] | Middle |
| HJ | Hijack | Middle |
| CO | [[cutoff|Cutoff]] | Late |
| BTN | [[button|Button]] | Late |
| SB | Small Blind | Blinds |
| BB | Big Blind | Blinds |

Before the flop, UTG acts first and the BB acts last. After the flop the order changes: the SB acts first and the **button acts last**.

## Why acting last wins money

When you act after your opponent, you see what they did before you decide. That's called being [[in-position|in position]]. It lets you:

- bet when they show weakness and check when they show strength
- control how big the pot gets
- bluff more often, because you see when they're weak

Players [[out-of-position|out of position]] have to guess. Over thousands of hands, the button wins the most money and the blinds lose the most.

## The second reason: who is left behind you

The more players still to act behind you, the more likely one of them holds a monster.

Any one player is dealt JJ or better, or AK, about 3% of the time. With **8 players behind you at UTG**, the chance that at least one of them has such a hand is roughly **1 in 5**. From the **button**, with only the blinds behind, it's about **1 in 17**.

> That's why early seats play only strong hands, and late seats can raise with much more.

## Key points

- Early seats: tight. Late seats: wide. The button is the best seat.
- Being in position is an advantage for the whole hand.
- The blinds are out of position after the flop, which makes them the hardest seats.
